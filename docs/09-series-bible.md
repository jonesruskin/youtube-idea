# 09 · Series bible: THE BLINK FILES (v5)

> **One impossible year, seen by a different person each time. Every clue is real physics. Every episode has its own twist.**

## The format in one paragraph
**Every File is a new story.** A different event or mystery, a different family, a different city, a different
title and twist; nothing carries over except the quality bar (docs/00 S11, premise registry `docs/premises.md`).
What every File shares: a kid or family at the centre, told by one of them later in the first person; every clue is
real science, researched and sourced; one twist, set up by what the characters do; complete in one episode.
File 001: the Sun goes dark for a year (an alien brake). File 002: a new volcano rises under Auckland in four days.

## Rules every File follows
1. **Standalone first.** The logline, the first 30 seconds and the ending must work without any other File (the
   *cold-viewer test* in each screenplay doc). No "previously on". Anything a new viewer needs is given in-story in
   under 10 seconds.
2. **A named narrator, in the first person**, telling it later. The opening line says who they are and when ("I was
   eight the summer the Sun went out"). They only narrate what they saw or were told.
3. **One twist per File**, set up by what characters *do* (never by the narrator promising), paid off bigger than it
   was set up. The setups must survive a rewatch. Maharaja is the model.
4. **Viewer chronology.** Nothing on screen reveals what the characters haven't discovered yet (lint gate). Every time
   jump is labelled. A second timeline is allowed, but it is never narrated or dated until its reveal.
5. **Every scene is entered for a reason the viewer already heard**, and leaves with the reason for the next.
6. **Sizes are real.** Everything is in metres (`M()`); the height chart never changes. Children read younger than the
   child next to them. Nothing floats: seats are drawn under sitters, and riders are attached to carriers.
7. **Titles and thumbnails stand alone** ("The Sun Went Out", "The Rice Farmer"), never "Part 7". Numbers live in the
   description and playlist.

## Film language
- **Stage engine** (`src/env/sun/stage.tsx`): detailed sets at real scale; the camera frames wides, mediums and close-ups; faces auto-centre; multi-plane parallax; Dutch angles for tense beats.
- **Real Earth:** NASA Blue/Black Marble (`src/fx/PhotoGlobe.tsx`).
- **On-screen text:** callouts (≤20 per File), Day tags with the year bar, place/time tags, title and end cards, diegetic signs.
- **Transitions:** whip pans (both shots move), fades for time jumps, a white flash for shocks, a match cut for the reveal.

## Review gates (nothing ships until these pass)
- `lint-episode.ts`:
  - hook ≤5 s, average shot ≤3.7 s, no idle shot
  - a development every ≤30 s, ≥4 reveals
  - 120–170 wpm, ≤20 callouts
  - costume continuity (other timelines exempt), scale, extras
  - **chronology** (nothing shown before its reveal)
  - **first-person narrator**
  - **silent second timeline**
- Whisper WER on every line (`qa_voice.py`); audio gates on the final file (`qa_audio.py`); motion gate (`qa_motion.py`).
- GroundLab, ScaleLab and contact sheets of every shot, reviewed by eye: nothing floating, ages read right, every clue placed.

## v5 canon (File 001), fixed for every later File (full model: docs/16-sun01-world-model.md)
- **The shell** was built long ago by the people of **HD 219134** (a real orange dwarf 21.3 ly away in Cassiopeia, five
  or more known planets): a **harbour brake** at our Sun, so a ship could one day stop here. Its slats sit edge-on, almost
  invisible; in total-eclipse photos (1919 Sobral and others) some show as straight lines that point to their star: a
  signpost, "if you can see this, call us". How it was built is the series' first open question.
- **1983:** Haruki Ito (30) traces the lines, is refused by the Council, and knocks anyway (1, 2, 3). The knock reaches
  HD 219134 in 2004; the builders set off at once, at nearly the speed of light.
- **Day 0 (summer 2026), 6:52 local (UTC+1):** the arriving ship switches the shell on. Every slat turns, focusing all
  the Sun's light into one beam that pushes against the ship's sail from the front: a brake. Earth goes dark (the day
  side at once; the night side wakes to no morning).
- **The world:** power, phones and internet keep running (coal, gas, nuclear, hydro); electric trains carry food, fuel and
  patients; planes are grounded and diesel trucks stop below about −30 °C; one big snowfall in week one, then frost.
  −30 °C by Day 7, −50 °C by Day 100, −70 °C by Day 365; underground stays about +10 °C. Cities move into metro shelters;
  underground farms grow lettuce, potatoes and mushrooms. Not everyone survives.
- **Day 2:** infrared telescopes find a warm sphere twice the Sun's width. **Day 300:** astronomers find the beam, aimed
  at HD 219134, and something inside it, slowing; it will stop in 65 days.
- **Day 365, 6:31:** the nearly stopped ship sends 1,679 pulses (23 × 73, the Arecibo format): the count to ten
  (continuing Ito's 1-2-3), a builder beside a small human, their star, their ship parked beside our Sun, Earth, and an
  empty square. **6:47:** while governments argue, Mira's drawing (a sun, a girl under it) goes out from Ito's dish.
  **6:52:** the ship stops; the brake is no longer needed; the Sun returns on the day side at once (New York, at night,
  gets an ordinary sunrise hours later). **7:03:** their answer to Mira: one, two, three.
- **Grace Okafor (Mum)**, a nurse at St Anne's, is held at the hospital by the Day 0 emergency, sleeps there and
  video-calls the kids every night at seven; on Day 30 the hospital opens a clinic in Harbour Street station and she
  works there, with the family, from then on.
- Open questions the series owns: how the shell was built; what the builders say next (their 7:03 reply to Mira is
  the hook of File 2); whether they knew a year of darkness would hurt us.
