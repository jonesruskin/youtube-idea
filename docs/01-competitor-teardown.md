# 01 · Competitor teardown: Ifverse and IFOLOGY

Data was pulled from the channel pages and RSS feeds on **21 Oct 2026**. Both channels launched in early October 2026, and both were monetized within about 2–3 weeks.

**Sequels decay.** Each part of a series earned much less than the one before:
- reaction-time series: 4.25M → 599K → 345K
- 500 IQ series: 1.15M → 381K → 180K

Each new part lost 50–85% of the previous part's views. **A new premise is what gets discovered. A sequel only keeps the viewers you already have.**

**Length is not what drives the views.** The 4.25M video is 7:13. IFOLOGY's runtimes grew from 3:30 to 22:06, but its views did not grow with them.

**Shorts work as trailers.** Each Short got 14–36K views and linked straight to its full video.

## What actually works (the principles worth learning)

1. **The viewer imagines owning the power.** Titles say "You", not "a man". The viewer is the hero before they click.
2. **A concrete number makes it vivid.** "10x", "500 IQ" and "100x" turn a vague wish into a picture.
3. **The first 5 seconds are the payoff.** The fly caught in a coffee cup, the 31-day phone battery. The hook is the reward, shown first.
4. **The progression goes: easy life, then being great at things, then the cost, then a choice.** Wish-fulfilment comes first, and stakes come in later.
5. **Real science adds credibility.** "World Athletics counts any start under 0.100s as a false start." A fact the viewer can repeat makes the fantasy feel earned.
6. **Recurring mysteries keep people watching across videos.** IFOLOGY has "Project IF" and "the woman in the gray coat", and each video ends by pointing at the next.
7. **Comment prompts are built into the description.** For example: "Would you wear the gloves?"
8. **Thumbnails are readable at a glance.** One character, one object, 1–2 words.

## What we must NOT copy (the parts that make it theirs)

Viewers spot clones, and YouTube's July 2025 / July 2026 "inauthentic content" rules demonetize **templated, look-alike** channels at the channel level. So we take none of these:

| Theirs | Ours |
|---|---|
| Black silhouette stickman in a hoodie, smug face | Flat colored characters with real skin tones, navy-ink outlines and a signature-color rim light |
| "What If You Could/Were [body power]" | "What If Aliens Left You [a thing]": inheritances, not body powers |
| Thumbnail of 1–2 caps words ending in a period ("TOO SLOW.") | Case-file thumbnail: torn paper strip, red **HEIR #NN** stamp, a curiosity phrase ("MOON BY 4AM") |
| Villain or event forces a binary moral choice ("the ball or the boy") | **The Rules**: three fine-print rules per inheritance. Tension comes from loopholes and clever thinking, not a forced dilemma |
| Hidden organization ("Project IF") and a mysterious recurring figure | **The Contest**: a rival legally contests the will in the open. The Executor is a funny alien estate lawyer, not a shadowy figure |
| A separate "Is any of this real?" science segment | **Executor's Footnotes**: numbered pop-up notes woven into the story as it happens |
| Part 1/2/3 trilogies | 12 standalone episodes held together by one sealed mystery (Item Thirteen) |

The anti-clone rule: if a frame, title or beat could be mistaken for theirs, redo it.
