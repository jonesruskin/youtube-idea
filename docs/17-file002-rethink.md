# 17 · File 002 rethink (after the CEO's v5.3 review)

> CEO: "There is no substance or good elements in the second video… There are no twists and turns in the story like at
> least the first video has." This page says why that happened and what replaces it. **Nothing gets built from this page
> until the CEO picks a premise** (two File 002s have now been rejected at the concept level after a full build).

## Why the volcano File failed (first principles)

| What makes File 001 work | Volcano File |
|---|---|
| An impossible image in the first 20 seconds (stars at breakfast) | Breakfast, then a creek thermometer. The event arrives at 8:40 of an 8:52 film |
| A question the viewer needs answered (who built it? why?) | No mystery. "Where will it come up?" is answered by a map at the midpoint |
| A second thread that pays off as a twist (1983 → "To me.") | One straight line: clue, clue, clue, phone call, evacuation, eruption |
| Somebody is hiding something (Ito) | Nobody wants anything the others oppose. Mum says "busy" once, then agrees |
| Planet-scale stakes shown through one family | One suburb. The city is told it is safe on screen |
| A "Day 1 to Day 365" clock (the proven format in `docs/08`) | Four days, no escalation the viewer can count |

It is a well-behaved science lesson. `docs/08` (our own niche scan) says the open lane is **cosmic disasters told as
survival timelines**; the volcano is neither cosmic nor a timeline. That is a premise error, and no amount of polish on
dialogue, voices or chairs fixes a premise.

## What every File must have from now on (new story gates, S13–S16)

- **S13. The hook is the event.** The impossible thing is on screen within 30 seconds, before anyone is introduced.
- **S14. A mystery with an owner.** One question the audience cannot answer, and one character who knows more than
  they say. The answer changes what earlier scenes meant.
- **S15. A twist ladder.** At least four reversals, each written as "we thought X, but Y", the last one inside the
  final two minutes. (The linter already counts `twist`; the treatment must list them before any build.)
- **S16. A clock the viewer can count** (days, distance, depth) with the world visibly worse at each step.

## Three candidate premises

### A (recommended) · "The Moon Got Closer Every Night. Day 1 to Day 200"
- **Hook (0:00–0:25):** a girl on a beach at night; the Moon rises twice its normal size; the sea follows it up the
  street. "The first night, we thought it was beautiful."
- **Clock:** the Moon's distance, counting down on screen. Tides, quakes and the length of the month change at each step
  (tides grow with the cube of closeness: real, checkable science).
- **Mystery and owner:** the girl's grandmother keeps the harbour's tide log (one family, 140 years of pencil numbers).
  She stops letting anyone see it.
- **Twist ladder (draft):**
  1. We thought it started on Day 1, but the tide log shows the sea has been arriving early for three years.
  2. We thought the Moon was falling, but it is being slowed: something dark and heavy is passing, and nobody can see it.
  3. We thought the danger was the Moon hitting Earth, but it can never hit: at about 18,000 km Earth's pull tears it
     apart (the Roche limit, real).
  4. We thought the grandmother was hiding the log out of fear, but she had already sent the numbers to every
     observatory three years ago. Nobody answered. (Her "They didn't believe pencil.")
  5. Final image: the first night with no Moon, and a ring across the whole sky. "We don't have a Moon any more. We
     have rings."
- **Why it can travel:** the thumbnail is one image a child understands (a giant Moon over a flooded street, or Earth
  with rings); the title is the proven Day-N format; every beat is a new picture.
- **Honest risk:** the cause in twist 2 needs a physics model that survives the question bank (stage 2 of the pipeline).
  If it does not survive, twist 2 changes; the rest stands.

### B · "The Sky Turned Red and Everything Electric Died. Day 1 to Day 365"
A Carrington-class solar superstorm (1859 and 2012 are real). Most relatable, weakest mystery; and File 001 already
used an aurora.

### C · Keep the volcano, rebuilt as a thriller: "They Evacuated 40,000 People to the Wrong Place"
Cold open on the eruption; the evacuation centre itself sits on the vent; Mum drew the wrong circle and will not hear it.
Cheapest (reuses every set), but still one suburb and no Day-N clock.

## Recommendation
**A.** It restores what File 001 has (impossible image, a hidden log, a knower, a clock, a last-minute reversal) with a
completely different event, family, place and ending. Build order: world model and question bank first, then a one-page
screenplay beat sheet for sign-off, then sets. The volcano File is archived like the earlier sea File; its engine work
(autoSplit, delivery, gates) stays.

## About "will it go viral?"
Nobody can promise that, and I will not pretend to. What the data in `docs/08` supports: this format had the highest
demand in the scan and no direct competitor for the cosmic Day-N combination. What decides it in practice is the title,
the thumbnail and the first 30 seconds; those can be tested (three thumbnails and three titles are already produced per
File for YouTube's Test & Compare) and 30-second Shorts cut from the hook cost almost nothing. The first real signal is
7-day click-through and 30-second retention on File 001, which needs the ElevenLabs voice first.
