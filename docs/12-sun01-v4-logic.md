# File 001 v4: the skeptical-viewer sheet

Every question a commenter could reasonably ask, and the shot that answers it on screen. Shot ids are from
`episodes/sun01-the-sun-went-out/script.ts`. Only one question is left open, on purpose: *what will they say back?*

## The one sentence a viewer can repeat

> The shell is a telephone that people at a real star, HD 219134 (21 light-years away), left around our Sun long ago.
> In 1983 Mr. Ito saw its "call here" lines and dialled. In 2026 they picked up. To send everything it knew about us
> across 21 light-years, the shell borrowed the Sun's whole power for one year. Then it showed us who they are and
> left an empty square for our answer. Mira answered, and the Sun came back.

## The questions

### The Sun and the shell
| Question | Answer | Shot |
|---|---|---|
| Is it an eclipse? | No: eclipses last minutes; this doesn't end | `polite` |
| Is it a power cut? | No: the kitchen lamp still works | `lights-work` |
| Did the Sun explode or die? | No: Venus goes dark 12 minutes late and Jupiter 72 minutes late, so it happened *at* the Sun; the Sun's gravity is still there because the tide is normal | `venus-out`, `at-the-sun`, `jupiter-out`, `gauge`, `in-front` |
| How did the world find it? | Infrared: anything that blocks starlight gets warm. Space agencies see it on Day 2 | `press`, `press-sci` (Dyson 1960 in the description) |
| Who built it? | The builders at HD 219134, long before 1919. We see one of them in the reply picture | `nobody-did`, `photo1919`, `reply-them` |
| Why did nobody notice it before? | Edge-on slats block almost nothing (less than the Sun's own 0.1% flicker); you only see them as faint lines during total eclipses | `blinds` (science note) |
| Why are there lines at all? | They're a signpost: extend them and they point to one star. It's how you find the number to call | `obs-chart`, `explain-chart` |
| Why did it close? | Because Ito called. Their answer arrived on Day 0 and the shell started the call back | `telephone` |
| Where did the sunlight go? | Into a beam aimed at HD 219134: the Sun's power is what it takes to talk across 21 light-years | `beam-news`, `beam-star`, `needed-the-sun` |
| What was it sending? | Everything it had ever heard from Earth: a hundred years of radio and TV | `everything`, `a-hundred-years` |
| Why one year? | That was the size of the upload; on Day 365 the beam stops | `from-the-sun`, `waiting` |
| Why didn't it open after the year? | It's holding the line: the reply ends with an empty square, waiting for our answer | `empty-square`, `our-turn`, `waiting` |
| Why did it open? | We answered. Their machine took Mira's picture (a sun, a girl standing under it) and turned the slats back | `drawing-grid`, `space-open` |
| Why did the light take 16:40? | 8 min 20 s for the signal to reach the shell, 8 min 20 s for sunlight to come back | `eight-eight`, `countdown-1` |
| Why does it come back as a sunrise? | They timed it: sent at 6:35 so the light arrives at 6:52, the same minute it went out. Their city is on the morning side | `send-time`, `same-minute` |
| Why does the whole day side light at once? | Light from the Sun reaches the whole day side within a fraction of a second | `globe-return` |

## Continuity rules this version enforces (lint gates)
- A prop changes hands only by `give` / `take` / `put`. Anything put on a table, desk or floor stays drawn there (`items`), and anything picked up is visible until the hand reaches it.
- Held props match across every cut in the same place and day (`timeJump` marks a deliberate time cut).
- No automatic hand-waving. Gestures are scripted per line, at most one line in three per character.
- Speakers are in their own shot. Two people keep their screen sides (180° rule).
- Costumes match the day and the scene.
