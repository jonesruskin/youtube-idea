# File 001: world model and question bank (v5)

Every scene must obey this file. Any line, picture or prop that contradicts it is a bug.
The **question bank** lists every question a first-time viewer could ask. Each one is answered on screen (shot id) or
marked **OPEN (series)** on purpose. Only two questions may stay open.

## 1. The premise in one causal chain
1. Long ago, the builders (from HD 219134, a real star 21.3 light-years away in Cassiopeia) left a **shell** of slats around our Sun.
   - The slats are edge-on, so the shell is almost invisible.
   - It is a **harbour brake**: a way to stop a ship arriving here.
   - Some slats form a **signpost**: lines that a civilisation photographing total eclipses could trace back to the builders' star.
2. In 1983 Mr. Ito, aged 30, found the lines on old eclipse plates (1919 Sobral and others). He traced them to HD 219134 and, against orders, sent a radio knock: one, two, three.
3. The knock reached HD 219134 in 2004 (21 years). They set off at once, at nearly the speed of light.
4. A ship moving that fast cannot carry enough fuel to stop. It needs a push from the front, and only a whole star's light pushes hard enough. On Day 0 (summer 2026, 6:52 a.m. local), the shell turned every slat and focused **all the Sun's light into one beam aimed at the incoming ship**. Earth went dark.
5. Braking took one year. They timed it to exactly one of our years (they have been watching us).
6. On Day 365 at 6:52 the ship stopped, parked beside the Sun. The brake was no longer needed, so the slats turned back edge-on and the Sun returned.
7. Just before the stop, at 6:31, the ship sent a picture: 1,679 pulses (23 × 73, the same trick as our own 1974 Arecibo message). It shows:
   - counting from 1 to 10, continuing Ito's 1-2-3;
   - the builders, and a human beside them;
   - their star;
   - their ship beside our Sun;
   - an empty square: your turn.
8. While governments argued, Mira sent her drawing at 6:47. It reached the ship 8 minutes later. Their answer reached us 8 minutes after that, at 7:03: one, two, three. That is the series tag; the episode's story is already complete.

## 3. The world without sunlight (real physics; every scene obeys it)

| Light travel | Sun→Earth 8 min 20 s. Venus (near the far side of the Sun, low in the east) goes dark 12 min later (7:04). Jupiter about 72 min later (8:04) | mirror, ito-predict, venus-out, jupiter-predict |

## 5. Question bank (each answered on screen, or OPEN on purpose)
### A. Premise mechanics
1. Why did the Sun go dark? The shell turned all its light into a brake beam. (blinds, closed-them, brake)
2. Is the Sun gone? No: the tide proves its gravity is still there. (gauge, still-there)
3. Where did the light go? Into one beam, aimed at the incoming ship. (beam-news, beam-space)
4. Who built the shell? The builders from HD 219134, long ago. (who-built)
5. *How* did they build it? **OPEN (series):** "Nobody knows how. Only that it's very old." (who-built)
6. How old is it? At least as old as the 1919 photo. (photo1919)
7. Why build it? A harbour, so they could stop here one day. (who-built)
8. How is it controlled from 21 light-years away? It isn't: the arriving ship switched it on. (brake)
9. Why the whole Sun? Stopping something that fast needs a push from the front, and only a star pushes that hard. (brake)
10. Why one year? Braking takes that long. They timed it to exactly one of our years. (brake, prediction)
11. Why give it back? The ship has stopped, so the brake is no longer needed. (now-stopped, sunrise)
12. How did Ito find them? Lines in eclipse photos traced back to one star. (obs-plate, obs-chart, explain-chart)
13. Why were the lines there? A signpost: "if you can see this, call us". (signpost)
14. Why only in eclipse photos? Edge-on slats show only when the corona backlights them. (blinds)
15. Why didn't modern telescopes find it before? Nearly invisible while edge-on; it only started to glow once closed. (press-sci)
16. Why did Ito knock? Curiosity and conviction, against orders. (obs-letter, explain-knock)
17. Why one, two, three? Counting shows a mind, not nature. (explain-knock)
18. How do we know the reply is to Ito? It continues his count to ten. (count-on)
19. How long did his knock take? 21 years. (explain-years)
20. How long did they take to get here? 23 years, including a year of braking. (explain-years)
21. How do we receive their picture "now"? The ship is here, 8 light-minutes away. (a-reply, explain-years)
22. Why 1,679 pulses? 23 × 73: a grid. The trick we used ourselves in 1974. (primes)
23. Why a picture? No shared language; pictures are universal. (a-picture)
24. What does the empty square mean? Your turn. (empty-square, our-turn)
25. Why did nobody find the beam until Day 300? It points away from us and is visible only where it lights up dust; it took months of searching. (beam-news)
26. How do we know something is in the beam? It glows where the beam hits it, and it is slowing down. (beam-news)
27. How did the world know when it would stop, and why go up to look? Its slowing rate ("stops in 65 days"), and scientists hope the light returns then. (beam-stop, day365)
28. How does Ito know the exact minute? They started at 6:52; he bets on exactly one year. (prediction)
29. Did they know it would hurt us? Ito doesn't know. Left honest, not a hole. (hurt-us)
30. What will they say next? **OPEN (series):** the tag is one, two, three. (hook)
31. Why didn't they warn us? A warning travels at light speed and would barely have beaten the ship. (warn)
32. Why is the Sun back for the night side later? It isn't late: it is night there. (ny-night)

### B. Physics and timing
33. Why does Venus go out later? Its light comes the long way, by reflection. (mirror)
34. Why 7:04 exactly? The extra light path is about 12 minutes. (ito-predict)
35. Why does Jupiter go out an hour later? Much farther away. (jupiter-predict)
36. Why is the tide a third smaller without the Sun? The Sun supplies about a third of the tide. (a-third)
37. Why an aurora on Day 3? A solar storm already on its way before the shell closed. (aurora)
38. Why are the fish fine on Day 1? Water holds far more heat than air; the sea cools months more slowly. (fish)
39. Why is underground warm? Ground heat: about 10 °C a few metres down. (ground-heat)
40. How cold does it get? −20 °C by Day 7, −50 °C by Day 100, −70 °C on Day 365. (freeze, day100-cold, day365)
41. Why is it summer clothes on Day 0? It was summer. (kitchen-morning, day1-rush)
42. Why stars at breakfast? With the Sun dark, the day sky is a night sky. (stars-at-breakfast)
43. Why can people stand on the surface on Day 365? Full arctic gear (people work at −70 °C in Antarctica). (day365)
44. Why does the light return at 6:52 everywhere at once? Everywhere in daylight sees it at the same instant. (globe-return)
45. Why does Sydney get the Sun at 3:52 p.m.? It's 9 hours ahead and in daylight. (sun-beach)
46. Why is New York still dark? It's the middle of the night there. (ny-night)
47. Why do the drawing and the reply take 8 minutes each way? The ship is beside the Sun. (hook)
48. Why "twenty-one" for Ito? It's the distance he wrote on his 1983 chart. (obs-chart, twenty-one)
