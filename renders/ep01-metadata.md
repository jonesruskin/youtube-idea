THE REAL SCIENCE IN THIS EPISODE
(1) Betelgeuse is a red supergiant roughly 550 light-years away, about 760 times wider than the Sun — placed at the Sun, it would reach past the orbit of Mars. — Source: NASA / ESA; radius ≈ 760 R☉ ≈ 3.5 AU
(2) At the International Space Station's altitude (~400 km), Earth's gravity is still about 90% as strong as on the ground. — Source: g(h) = g₀·(R/(R+h))² with R = 6,371 km, h = 400 km → 0.89 g
(3) Newton's cannonball: fired fast enough (about 7.8 km/s near Earth), a projectile falls around the planet instead of into it — that continuous falling is what orbiting astronauts feel as weightlessness. — Source: Newton, A Treatise of the System of the World (1728); NASA
(4) Weight and mass are different: weight is the pull of gravity, mass is how much stuff there is. Remove the weight and the mass — and its inertia — stays. — Source: Newton's first law
(5) Newton's third law: every action has an equal and opposite reaction — the same principle that drives rockets and that astronauts use to move around in orbit. — Source: Newton's third law of motion
(6) Air pushes up on everything with a force equal to the weight of the air it displaces (buoyancy). With weight removed, that small upward push wins — slowly for dense objects, faster for hollow ones. — Source: Archimedes' principle
(7) Roughly 85–90% of a large rocket's launch mass is propellant — the Saturn V weighed about 2,900 tonnes at launch, nearly all of it fuel. — Source: NASA Saturn V fact sheet
