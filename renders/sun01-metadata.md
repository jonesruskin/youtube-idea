THE REAL SCIENCE IN THIS STORY
(1) Sunlight takes about 8 minutes 20 seconds (499 s) to travel 1 AU to Earth. — Source: NASA Solar System Exploration — "Sun: Facts"; IAU definition of the astronomical unit (149,597,870.7 km)
(2) The Moon shines only by reflected sunlight; it is about 1.3 light-seconds from Earth. — Source: NASA — "Moon: Facts" (average distance 384,400 km)
(3) Changes in gravity travel at the speed of light (confirmed by GW170817), so Earth keeps orbiting for ~8 minutes, then moves on in a straight line at its orbital speed of ~29.8 km/s. — Source: LIGO/Virgo + Fermi, ApJL 848 L13 (2017); NASA Earth Fact Sheet (mean orbital velocity 29.78 km/s)
(4) A resting adult releases roughly 80–100 W of heat. — Source: ASHRAE Handbook — Fundamentals, ch. 18 (heat gain from occupants); U.S. DOE
(5) Water expands about 9% when it freezes, which is why frozen pipes burst. — Source: USGS Water Science School — "Ice, snow and glaciers"
(6) The solar wind takes about 1–4 days to reach Earth; particles already in flight would keep arriving after the Sun vanished, driving aurorae. — Source: NOAA Space Weather Prediction Center — "Solar Wind"; "Aurora"
(7) Without sunlight, Earth’s average surface temperature would fall below −18°C within about a week and toward −73°C within a year. — Source: Universe Today / Phys.org — "What would happen if the Sun disappeared?" (2015); NASA Earth energy budget
(8) Burning fuel indoors produces carbon monoxide, an odourless, colourless gas that can kill. — Source: U.S. CDC — "Carbon Monoxide Poisoning: Prevention"
(9) A few metres down, ground temperature stays near the yearly average; deeper, rock warms by ~25–30°C per km (the geothermal gradient). — Source: British Geological Survey — "Geothermal energy"; U.S. EIA — "Geothermal explained"
(10) The body clock (circadian rhythm) is set mainly by light; without a daily light signal it drifts. — Source: U.S. NIH, National Institute of General Medical Sciences — "Circadian Rhythms" fact sheet
(11) Ice is less dense than water, so a freezing ocean forms a floating, insulating lid; models show oceans on a sunless Earth would stay liquid beneath thick ice for a very long time. — Source: D. J. Stevenson, "Life-sustaining planets in interstellar space?", Nature 400, 32 (1999); NOAA — "Why does ice float?"
(12) Earth's atmosphere holds ~1.2 × 10^18 kg of oxygen; all humans breathe ~2.5 × 10^12 kg a year — enough for well over a thousand centuries. — Source: NASA Earth Fact Sheet (atmospheric mass 5.1 × 10^18 kg, 20.95% O2 by volume); human O2 use ~0.84 kg/day (NASA life-support baseline)
(13) An adult needs roughly 2,000 calories a day. — Source: U.S. Dietary Guidelines for Americans 2020–2025
(14) Moving in a straight line at 29.8 km/s from 1 AU, Earth would be ~2 AU from the Sun’s old position after 100 days (Mars orbits at 1.52 AU) and ~6.4 AU after a year (Jupiter: 5.2 AU). — Source: Calculation from NASA Planetary Fact Sheets (orbital velocity and semi-major axes)
(15) Hydrothermal vent ecosystems are powered by chemosynthesis — microbes using chemicals from the vents, not sunlight. — Source: NOAA Ocean Exploration — "What is a hydrothermal vent?"; Woods Hole Oceanographic Institution
