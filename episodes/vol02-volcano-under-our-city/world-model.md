# File 002 · World model: "A Volcano Started Growing Under Our City"

A new story, unrelated to File 001 (docs/00 S11): no sky event, no aliens, no year. Four days in Auckland, a real
city built on a real volcanic field, when a new volcano starts to come up. Everything below is real science;
the eruption itself is fiction set in a place where it could really happen.

## 1. The premise in one causal chain
1. Auckland is built on a volcanic field of about 53 volcanoes. Each eruption opens at a brand-new place, so nobody
   can say in advance where the next one will open. The youngest, Rangitoto, erupted about 600 years ago.
2. On Monday, a swarm of small earthquakes starts deep under the city: magma is rising. Research says the warning
   may be only a few days, and the city's noise makes small quakes hard to pin down, so scientists can't yet say
   where it will break through.
3. Ana (12) keeps a notebook for her class's creek project and notices three small things on Tuesday:
   - the creek is warm and fizzing;
   - her brother's goldfish are dead in their low backyard pond;
   - her dad's bus scrapes on a hump in the road that wasn't there last week.
   Rising magma lets out gas first (CO₂, heavier than air, collects in low places and in water) and pushes the
   ground up.
4. She marks them on a map: all three are within a kilometre of her own street, not where the official estimate is.
   Her mum, a volcano scientist on the monitoring team, sends a gas team and checks the nearest GPS station, and
   the instruments confirm it.
5. The evacuation zone is redrawn on Wednesday to include their suburb. Dad drives evacuation buses, and 40,000
   people are out by Thursday evening.
6. Thursday night the new volcano opens in the park at the end of their street. Nobody inside the zone is hurt.
   Their house is gone.
7. A year later the city has 54 volcanoes, and the newest is where Ana's bedroom used to be.

### A. Premise mechanics
1. Who tells the story? Ana, grown up; she was twelve (creek-mon, ana-now)
2. Where is it set? Auckland, a city built on about fifty volcanoes (field-map, newest)
3. Why can't anyone say where the next volcano will open? Almost every one erupted once, each in a new place (field-map, nobody-knew, lesson)
4. What starts it? A swarm of small earthquakes as magma rises (quake-school, swarm, alert)
5. How deep is the magma at first? About ten kilometres (swarm, mum-call)
6. Why can't the scientists pin it down? City noise blurs small earthquakes (cant-pin)
7. Where did the scientists first guess? Under the harbour (mum-tv, zone)
8. What is Ana's first clue? The creek warms from 14 to 19 degrees and fizzes (creek-tue, notebook)
9. What is the second clue? The goldfish die in the pond in the lowest part of the garden (pond, pond-low)
10. What is the third clue? The road on Kōwhai Street is rising; Dad's bus scrapes (bus-scrape, dinner-tue)
11. Why do those clues matter? Gas comes out ahead of magma and sinks into hollows; the ground above rises (co2, uplift)
12. How does Ana know they point to her street? She maps them; all three are within a kilometre of home (ana-map)
13. Does she check before telling anyone? Yes: 21 degrees at night, warm air from a crack in the hump (check-it, night-creek, warm-road)
14. Why does Mum listen? The details are specific, and they match what the instruments can test (ana-insists, ana-insists-2, mum-listens)
15. How do the scientists confirm it? A GPS station has risen 4 cm; the gas team's meters go off (confirm, gas-team)
16. What changes because of Ana? The evacuation zone moves onto her suburb (zone, callback)
17. Where does the volcano open? In the park at the end of her street (park-steam, our-street)
18. When? Thursday at 11:52 PM (eruption)
19. Is anyone in the zone hurt? No (news-homes, headcount)
20. What does the family lose? Their house (ours-gone, crater-edge)
21. What happens to the volcano? It builds a hill for two months, then stops for good (weeks, one-year)
22. Will it ever erupt again? Auckland volcanoes are almost always one-offs (lesson, weeks)

### B. Physics and timing
23. How fast does the magma rise? About two and a half kilometres a day (swarm, hourly)
24. How long from the first quakes to the eruption? About four days (title, eruption)
25. Why does the creek fizz? Volcanic gas (CO2) bubbling through the groundwater (co2, creek-tue)
26. Why does the creek warm up? Gas and heat coming up ahead of the magma (notebook, night-creek)
27. Why did only the pond fish die? CO2 is heavier than air and pools in the lowest places (co2, pond-low)
28. Why does the road bulge? Rising magma pushes the ground up (uplift, bus-scrape)
29. How much did the ground rise? About 4 cm in a day at the GPS station (confirm)
30. Why does it explode at first? Magma meets groundwater and flashes it to steam (surge)
31. What is the cloud that races out? Ash and rock rushing along the ground (surge, nobody-there)
32. Why is the evacuation zone kilometres wide? The first blasts reach kilometres out (surge, zone)
33. Why did the boom come later than the flash? Sound takes about 23 seconds to travel 8 km (counting, boom)
34. How far is the hall from the volcano? About eight kilometres, outside the zone (evac-centre, boom)
35. What is the depth on Thursday afternoon? Less than a kilometre (very-close, hourly)
36. Why does the duck pond steam? Heat and gas reach the surface just before the vent opens (park-steam)
37. What did Rangitoto do? It rose out of the sea about 600 years ago (rangitoto, footprints-tell)
38. Is Rangitoto still dangerous? It is the youngest; families climb it (rangitoto)
39. How long does an Auckland eruption last? Weeks to months (weeks)
40. Why does ash close the airport? Ash damages jet engines and covers runways (ash-city)
