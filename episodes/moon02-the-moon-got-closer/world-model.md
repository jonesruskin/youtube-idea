## 1. The premise in one causal chain
1. A primordial black hole, a third as heavy as the Moon and smaller than a grain of sand, drifts in from deep space and is caught between the Earth and the Moon. It gives off no light. **This capture is the File's one impossible thing; everything after it is ordinary physics.**
2. Each time it loops past the Moon, the two trade speed, exactly as a spacecraft does in a gravity slingshot: the visitor gains, the Moon loses.
3. A slower Moon cannot hold its high orbit, so it sinks closer to the Earth, a little more with every loop.
4. A closer Moon pulls harder on the sea: the tide-raising pull grows with the cube of closeness.
5. After 200 days the visitor has gained enough speed to escape the Earth, and it leaves. Nothing is stealing the Moon's speed any more, so the Moon stays in its new, low orbit: 128,000 km instead of 384,000 km.
6. The tides stay 27 times higher, for good. People move uphill and learn the new sea.

**Energy check (why the visitor must be that heavy):** lowering the Moon from 384,000 to 128,000 km releases about 7.6 × 10²⁸ J. A visitor of 2.4 × 10²² kg carrying that away leaves at about 2.5 km/s, which is the escape speed from the Earth at 128,000 km. A lighter visitor would have to leave far faster than it could ever be while still bound; so "a third of the Moon" is the mass the story's own numbers require. Its event horizon is about 0.04 mm across.

## 2. Timeline arithmetic

Low tides fall as far as high tides rise: by Day 150 the harbour is dry sand for kilometres, twice a day.
The Roche limit (where a moon is pulled apart) is about 18,000 km: seven times closer than the Moon ever comes.

## 3. The world's systems (real numbers; every scene obeys them)
| System | Rule |
|---|---|
| Physics of the premise | §1. The visitor is never seen. Its own pull adds a small extra tide that does not follow the Moon (a third of the Moon's mass): that is what Nana's log catches on Day 3 and what scientists use to find it |
| Who can measure it | The Moon's distance is known to the centimetre every night (lunar laser ranging), so the world knows from Day 1 that the Moon is closer. What nobody knows until Day 75 is why, and until then nobody can say where it stops |

| Ground | The solid Earth's own tide (30 cm normally) reaches 1.8 m by Day 120 and 8 m at the end. Small earthquakes become weekly from Day 100; no great quake or volcano in this story's region |
| Sky | The Moon is 3× wider and 9× brighter at the end. Nights with the Moon up are bright enough to read by |
| Weather | Unchanged for this story (no storm is needed; the tide is the danger) |
| Power, phones, internet | Work all the way through: inland power stations and the grid are untouched; coastal plants shut down in order |
| Transport | Coast roads and railways close from Day 60. Inland roads work. The lifeboat station moves uphill on Day 95 and launches from a slip cut into the hill |
| Jobs and school | The fishing fleet is laid up by Day 60. School moves to the hill on Day 70. People work on the move uphill |
| Food and water | Shops inland stay stocked; the reservoir is on high ground. Nobody starves in this story |
| Human cost | Said once, plainly: coastal cities everywhere had months to leave, and most people did; not everyone could |
