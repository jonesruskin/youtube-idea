# Episode 5 creative brief: Apophis, 13 April 2029

Ep4 promised: "On April thirteenth, twenty twenty-nine, an asteroid called Apophis will fly closer to Earth than many
of our satellites. Next time: what that night will look like from the ground."

The trap: every Apophis video on YouTube is the same video. "Will it hit us?", a 2004 scare, a countdown, a red
risk corridor, "relax, it will miss". The answer is known (it misses: JPL, radar 2021), so a "will it hit" video is
a video with no question in it. Our own back catalogue adds two more traps: Ep1 and Ep3 were "an intruder arrives,
watch what it does to us". Doing that again with Apophis would be a third copy.

What the data says that nobody builds a video around (JPL Horizons, our `model.py`):
- **For once, the asteroid is the one in danger.** Earth bends Apophis's path by 27°, speeds it from 5.9 to 7.4 km/s,
  and rewrites its orbit for good: its year goes from 323.5 days to 423.2 days, from 59 % of its time inside Earth's
  orbit to 28 %. Its tumble may halve or double (Benson et al. 2023); landslides are possible (Yu et al. 2014).
  Earth does not notice. That is the story: a near miss told from the side of the small one.
- **The view from the rock is astonishing and true.** At closest approach Earth is 19.3° wide from Apophis (37 full
  Moons). It rises as a dark night side lit by cities (phase angle 159° at 19:00 UTC), is half lit at closest (97°),
  and leaves gibbous (56°).
- **The asteroid as a place is absurd in a way people can feel:** a 70 kg person weighs 0.9 g (less than a paper
  clip), a pebble dropped from shoulder height takes 2.6 minutes to land, a jump faster than 20 cm/s never comes down.
- **The ground view is gentle:** a 3rd-magnitude point of light crossing a fist of sky in ten minutes over Lisbon
  (Horizons topocentric ephemeris), seen by perhaps two billion people.

## Story shapes considered
1. **The scare, replayed** (2004: the odds climb to 1 in 37, then vanish). Rejected as the spine: it is every other
   Apophis video, and its ending is "relax". Kept as one short chapter, because the odds climbing *is* dramatic.
2. **The countdown night**, minute by minute from Lisbon. Rejected as the spine: it is Ep4's one-place clock again.
3. **Shot / reverse shot (chosen):** the same night told from both ends of a 32,000 km line, the stargazer's sky and
   the asteroid's sky, cross-cut until closest approach, with the twist that the *rock* is the one at risk.
4. **The truck and the bicycle**: the physics of a near miss, the big body unaware, the small body shoved and spun.
   Kept as the explanation inside shape 3 (the tides chapter).
5. **Ride with the probe** (ESA's Ramses beside the asteroid). Rejected as the spine: a ride-along is Ep2's point of
   view; kept for one chapter, because Ramses really will fly alongside.

## Chosen
Shape 3 with shape 4 inside it. A role reversal: the near miss from the small body's side. The episode cross-cuts
between two skies, a rooftop sky in which a new moving star appears, and the asteroid's sky, in which a planet 37
Moons wide swells, turns from night to half day, pulls, and leaves. The ending is a mirror: each world becomes a
fading moving star in the other's sky, and only one of them is changed.

## Unlike earlier episodes
- **Story shape:** a role reversal (the planet is the danger, the asteroid the victim), told shot / reverse shot.
  No intruder that wrecks Earth (Ep1, Ep3), no descent (Ep2), no single place on one clock (Ep4).
- **Narrator:** first person plural, future tense ("we will see"): a forecast of a night that has not happened yet.
  Ep1-Ep3 spoke to "you", Ep4 was third person present. A new voice, too (British, Kokoro bf_emma; Ep1-Ep4: af_heart).
- **Point of view:** standing on the asteroid's surface (curved horizon, boulders, black sky) against rooftops on
  Earth. No river, no orbital Solar-System overview of an invader.
- **Time scale:** one evening counted in Universal Time, with one jump back to 2004 and one jump forward a year.
- **Ending:** a mirror shot, not odds and not a fossil bookend.
- **Palette:** basalt grey regolith, sodium-lamp gold city lights, one cyan planet. Ep3 was violet and ice, Ep4
  fern green and ember.
- **Thumbnail:** a round rear-view mirror standing on the asteroid's gravel, and in its glass the night side of Earth
  looming up from behind like a truck: an everyday object holding the role reversal. Not a calendar, not an
  underwater window, no text, no number.

## Open moment
Seeing a star that moves: a satellite gliding across the night sky, steady, not blinking like a plane. Most people
have seen one and wondered what it was. It *is* the episode: on 13 April 2029 a new moving star crosses the sky,
and it is passing below the satellites.

## Simplicity
- **Hero:** Apophis (named in most segments).
- **Villain:** Earth's gravity: the truck that does not notice the bicycle.
- **Room:** the space just outside Earth where the satellites fly, seen from the asteroid's surface and from the ground.
- **Always moving:** the moving star / the Earth growing in the asteroid's sky, on one Universal-Time clock.

## Every line shows what it says
Rooftops and a moving point for the ground view; a globe with the satellite shells for "closer than our satellites";
a blink comparator and an odds meter for 2004; the rock itself (two lobes, tumbling) beside the Eiffel Tower; the
asteroid's surface for weight, the dropped pebble and the jump; a road at dusk for the truck and the bicycle; a tide
diagram with the stretch; the orbit diagram from the Horizons vectors for the new year; a probe flying beside the rock.

## Everything inside the story
No title card, no chapter cards. The Betelgeuse teaser comes before the story's last beat (the mirror), then one
branded outro under 12 s.

## Anatomy reference
No animals appear. The one "creature" is the hero rock, and it is drawn from the real measurements:
**Apophis (99942 Apophis)**: elongated and probably bilobed (a "peanut"), about 450 m long and 340 m mean diameter;
grey Sq-type rock, probably a rubble pile with boulders and gravel; a slow non-principal-axis tumbler (spin about
264 h, precession about 27.4 h). Drawn as a two-lobed grey body with boulders and a smooth neck, never as a lumpy
potato with glowing cracks or as a burning fireball. The cyclist is a person on a road bike drawn side-on
(two wheels, frame, rider on the saddle).

## Honesty notes
- Landslides and the spin change are model predictions with wide error bars: say "may" and "could".
- Ramses is planned (ESA committed it in full in Nov 2025; launch April 2028): say "should arrive".
- "As wide as the Eiffel Tower is tall": 340 m mean diameter vs 330 m tower; true for the average, the long axis is ~450 m.
- Magnitude 2.8 at best from Lisbon (Horizons); "about as bright as the stars of the Big Dipper" (2nd mag) is fair, say "nearly".
