# Black hole episode: 17 views in 3 days. What it means, what we do.

## 1. Read the number correctly
The channel launched with one video, no subscribers and no watch history. YouTube has no audience to test it on, so the first
days are a trickle (single- or double-digit impressions are normal). **17 views tells us nothing about the video yet.**
What tells us something is in YouTube Studio → Analytics → this video → "Reach" and "Engagement":

| Metric | Healthy for a new channel | If it's below that, the problem is… |
|---|---|---|
| Impressions | 100s+ by day 7 | Not the video. The channel has no signal yet → more uploads + Shorts |
| Impressions CTR | ≥ 4–5 % | **Packaging** (thumbnail/title) → run the A/B test below |
| Average % viewed | ≥ 40 % | **The opening** → re-cut the first 45 s |
| Traffic source | Browse/Suggested growing | Still "External/Direct" only = YouTube hasn't started recommending it |

## 2. What I found reviewing the episode
1. **The thumbnail is the most generic image in the genre.** A black hole plus a half-Earth, a "doom countdown" line. Hundreds of
   videos look exactly like it. "30 YEARS LEFT" also reads like a fake news warning.
2. **Our one-of-a-kind finding is hidden.** Our own N-body simulation found that *the black hole leaves carrying Venus* and throws
   every planet out. Nobody else has that, and it isn't in the title or thumbnail.
3. **The opening is slow and dark.** The first frame is black. Then 27 s of night sky in Chile pass before we say "black hole",
   and a second title card ("THE DARK PASSAGE") follows at ~0:45. Our own rule (docs/01) is: *the first 5 seconds are the payoff*.
   On a phone, near-black frames with small mono text look empty.
4. **One video is a weak signal.** Episode 2 (Jupiter) is finished and not uploaded.

## 3. Actions, in order
**Today**
- Studio → this video → **Test & compare** with 3 thumbnails: current (A), `thumbnail_B_venus.png` ("IT TOOK VENUS"),
  `thumbnail_C_frozen.png` ("NO MORE SUN"). B is my pick.
- Change the title to the one that matches the winning thumbnail, e.g.
  **"A Black Hole Passed Through Our Solar System. It Took Venus."** (keep the current title as the fallback).
- Check settings: Made for kids = **No**, visibility Public, playlist added. A wrong "made for kids" flag kills distribution.
- Pin the comment, and reply to every comment in the first week (comments are a ranking signal).

**This week**
- **Upload Episode 2 (Jupiter)** within 2–3 days. Each finished video sitting unpublished costs discovery.
- **3 Shorts from Episode 1** (vertical re-renders from the same simulation, 30–45 s each), each linking to the full video:
  1. "A black hole passes the Sun. Watch what happens to Venus." (the capture, as a payoff loop)
  2. "The Moon gets pulled off Earth in 4 seconds." (the T-30 days chapter)
  3. "Earth after being thrown out: 0.04% sunlight." (the Wanderer ending)
  Shorts are the fastest way for a channel with zero subscribers to get impressions.

**Day 10 decision, from the data**
- CTR low, retention fine → keep testing packaging. The video is fine.
- Retention under 30 % at 0:45 → re-cut the opening (the payoff first: Venus captured in 5 s, then "Here's how we got here"),
  drop the second title card, re-upload. With 17 views, re-uploading costs nothing.
- Both fine but impressions are tiny → it's just the new-channel ramp. Keep a weekly cadence + daily Shorts.

## 4. Rule for every future episode
The thumbnail and title must carry **the one result only our simulation found** (Venus captured, ship stops at X km),
never the generic premise ("black hole + Earth"). The premise gets the click on big channels; the unique finding gets it on ours.
